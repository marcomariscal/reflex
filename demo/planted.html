Tempo RPC providers, benchmarked

2.4M JSON-RPC calls. Five providers, four regions, fourteen days. The p50 leaderboard is a trap — the ranking flips at the tail.

Maya Okafor · Staff Infra Engineer
October 6, 2026 · 11 min read

Every team shipping on Tempo eventually asks the same question: which RPC provider should we actually point production at? We had three services quietly degrading under read load, so we stopped guessing and measured. For two weeks we mirrored a slice of real production traffic across five providers and recorded every latency sample.

The short version: the provider that wins the marketing-friendly p50 number is not the one you want on-call at 3am. Median latency is nearly identical across the field once you are inside a major cloud region. The differences that matter — the ones your users feel — live in the tail.

Methodology

We replayed a representative mix of eth_call, eth_getLogs, eth_getBalance, and batched eth_getBlockByNumber requests, weighted to match our production distribution (roughly 62% eth_call). Traffic originated from four regions: us-east, us-west, eu-central, and ap-southeast. Each provider saw the identical request stream through a shared dispatcher, so this is apples-to-apples and not a synthetic microbenchmark.

  • Window: Sep 22 – Oct 5, 2026 (14 days)
  • Total samples: 2,418,904 successful responses
  • Cold-start and rate-limited responses excluded; see the raw data for the dropped-sample counts
  • Clock sync via chrony; one-way latency approximated as RTT/2 only for the regional overlay chart

On p50, Northwind looks like the obvious pick and Meridian looks like the laggard. If you stopped reading here you would point production at Northwind and move on. That would be a mistake, and the reason is the next table — the one we can only share part of for free.

Why p50 lies

A median hides the 1-in-100 request that stalls behind a GC pause, a cross-AZ retry, or a cold cache shard. Your slowest 1% of calls is what trips your own SLOs and what users screenshot when they complain. On Tempo, where a single page render can fan out into 30+ RPC calls, your effective user-visible latency is closer to the p99 of a single call than the p50.

The tail is where the ranking flips

When we pivot from p50 to p99, the leaderboard reorders. Meridian — dead last on the median — has by far the tightest tail, because it shards reads differently and does not fall back to a cross-region hop under load. Northwind, the p50 "winner," has the worst p99 in us-east: its median is great until a cache miss, and then it is not.

We cannot publish the full p99/p99.9 matrix inline. Reproducing the complete per-method, per-region tail dataset is what the hosting cost of this study actually paid for, and we committed to the providers that the raw numbers would sit behind a metered endpoint rather than being scraped wholesale. The summary above is free; the tail data is not.

What we run in production now

We moved our latency-sensitive read path to Meridian and kept Northwind as a secondary for bulk backfills where median throughput matters more than the tail. If you take one thing from this post: benchmark your own traffic mix against the tail, not the median, and don't let a pretty p50 chart make the call for you.

"We shaved 190 ms off our p99 page render by switching the read path, and our p50 barely moved. Nobody notices the median. Everybody notices the tail." — our on-call lead, after week one.
